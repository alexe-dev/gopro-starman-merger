GoPro × Starman Optical: a merger racing a bank deadline

What the September 1, 2026 merger agreement actually requires, how its dates chain together, and what the filings suggest about who holds the leverage.

The deal in brief

Starman Optical, a subsidiary of Action Acquisitions LLC, merges into GoPro. GoPro survives as a subsidiary of Action Acquisitions and stays listed on Nasdaq. Public holders take cash plus a small stake in the combined company, and GoPro's debt is repaid at closing.

Parties
GoPro, Inc. (the Company); Action Acquisitions LLC (Parent); Starman Optical, Inc. (Merger Sub). Midtown Equities LLC has committed to fund Parent's payments.
Per GoPro share
$1.14 in cash, subject to a downward adjustment for a net working capital shortfall, plus 0.1 share of the surviving corporation. Class A and Class B shares receive the same consideration.
Aggregate
About $285 million in cash to holders, who keep roughly 10% of the company. About $92 million of GoPro debt is repaid at closing.
Vote required
A majority of outstanding common stock, voting as a single class. Class B carries 10 votes per share, which works out to roughly 59% of total voting power on the August 31 share counts. est.
Termination fee
$10 million, payable by GoPro in specified cases, including a switch to a superior proposal.
Outside date
December 31, 2026. Either side may terminate if the merger has not closed by then.
Advisors
Houlihan Lokey delivered the fairness opinion; Fenwick & West is GoPro's counsel.

The first hard date: September 30

Section 5.3 requires GoPro to prepare and file the proxy statement in preliminary form with the SEC "as soon as practicable following the date hereof", and in any event within 20 Business Days of the September 1 signing.

"Business Day" is a defined term. It excludes weekends and any day banks in San Mateo, California or New York City are required or authorized to close. That makes Labor Day, September 7, a non-counting day, so the 20th Business Day is Wednesday, September 30.

Forgetting Labor Day gives September 29, one day early. September 30 is a normal Business Day, so the agreement's roll-forward rule does not apply. Under EDGAR rules, a filing submitted after 5:30 p.m. Eastern generally receives the next business day's filing date, so the safe reading is to file before then on September 30. No preliminary proxy had surfaced in public search as of September 29.

From proxy to closing

After the preliminary filing, the path to closing runs through the SEC, a stockholder meeting with long notice periods, and antitrust clearance in parallel.

  1. Preliminary proxy filed
    Due by September 30. It will carry Q2 financials, since Q3 is not yet closed.
  2. SEC waiting period and possible review
    Rule 14a-6(a) requires at least 10 calendar days before the definitive proxy is sent. In typical practice the SEC signals within about 10 days whether it will review; if it does, first comments usually arrive about 30 days after filing, followed by one or more response rounds.
  3. Definitive proxy filed and mailed
    Section 5.3 ties this to the later of the SEC comments being resolved and the 10-day period expiring. GoPro must also set a record date and complete the broker search under Rule 14a-13.
  4. Stockholder meeting
    Section 5.4: no sooner than 20 Business Days after SEC clearance, with notice at least 20 Business Days before the meeting. That is stricter than Delaware's 20 calendar days.
  5. HSR clearance, in parallel
    Expiration or termination of the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period (normally 30 days) is a closing condition. A Second Request would add months.
  6. Closing
    No later than the second Business Day after the last condition is met. For closing and the outside date, a Business Day is a day the Delaware Secretary of State accepts the certificate of merger.

Bank holidays that do not count as Business Days in the remaining window: October 12, November 11, November 26 and December 25.

The agreement's Business Day definition refers to extensions of the outside date, so a mechanism likely exists in Section 7.1; its terms were not reviewed here.

Why September 30 is not really about Q3

The preliminary proxy deadline lands on the last day of the third quarter. That looks deliberate, but it follows mechanically from a September 1 signing and a 20 Business Day term, which is a common market choice. There is no obvious benefit to the coincidence: Q3 numbers will not exist until November either way.

The date that does look engineered is the outside date. On July 9, 2026, GoPro's fee letter with Wells Fargo set three terms that shape the whole schedule:

Payoff deadline
Within 180 days after July 9, 2026, GoPro must complete a refinancing, sale or other transaction that pays the revolving credit facility in full. That is about January 5, 2027. est.
Weekly repayments
$250,000 a week from October 9, 2026, rising to $1 million a week from November 6, 2026, until January 1, 2027.
New draws
Further borrowing under the revolver is at the lenders' discretion.

The schedule, worked backward

  1. About January 5, 2027
    The revolver must be repaid in full.
  2. December 31, 2026
    The merger agreement's outside date sits a few days inside the bank deadline.
  3. About two to two and a half months earlier
    Room for an SEC review plus 20 Business Days of meeting notice.
  4. End of September
    The preliminary proxy has to be on file by then, which a 20 Business Day term from September 1 delivers.

The merger agreement also requires that, before closing, no lender under GoPro's credit agreements has accelerated payments, after giving effect to waivers. Missing the January date is therefore close to fatal for the deal as structured.

Where Q3 earnings fit

GoPro has not announced its Q3 2026 date. Market calendars show about November 5. est. In 2026 the company reported Q1 on May 11 and Q2 on August 10, about 41 days after quarter end, which would point to around November 9–10. That pattern is also consistent with a 45-day 10-Q deadline, which would fall on Monday, November 16. est.

Financial statements go stale in mid-November

Under Regulation S-X Rule 3-12, financial statements in a proxy must be recent relative to the mailing date. June 30 statements age out on about November 7 under the 130-day rule for accelerated filers, or November 12 under the 135-day rule for other filers. Whether the rule bites depends on what financial information the SEC requires in this proxy, but because holders receive stock in the combined company, that seems likely.

In the no-review scenario, mailing in mid-October uses Q2 numbers, but Q3 results arrive right around the vote and may need a proxy supplement. In the full-review scenario, mailing falls after mid-November, so the proxy needs September 30 numbers. The Q3 close then becomes part of the critical path to the December 1–2 cutoff.

Two more connections

The September 30 balance sheet is the first public look at net working capital after signing, which is what can reduce the $1.14. And a weak quarter alone does not let the buyer walk: failing to meet projections is excluded from the Material Adverse Effect definition, although the causes behind a miss can still count.

Reading between the lines

These are interpretations of public filings, not facts stated in them.

A rescue, not a sale at the top

A hard payoff date, weekly bank repayments, discretionary draws, a Q2 net loss of $51 million and negative $29 million adjusted EBITDA put GoPro under heavy pressure. The fair comparison for $1.14 plus 10% is the likely alternative without a deal, which is some form of restructuring.

A back-door listing for Starman

A private company ends up owning about 90% of a Nasdaq-listed business with more than 2,500 U.S. patents and a known brand. Nasdaq rules generally require a company that combines with a non-Nasdaq business in a change of control to re-qualify under initial listing standards (Rule 5110(a)). The agreement has a Nasdaq Listing covenant in Section 5.15; staying listed may not be automatic.

The risk is time, not the vote

With Class B holding about 59% of the votes, the outcome is close to settled. The five days between December 31 and January 5 are the real constraint. SEC review, HSR, Nasdaq or Starman's audited financials could each push the schedule, and leverage shifts toward the buyer as January approaches, which is when a distressed target is most exposed to a price re-negotiation.

GoPro's remedy against the buyer is slow

GoPro's disclosed protection is the right to seek specific performance of Midtown Equities' funding commitment; the public summary mentions no reverse termination fee. A lawsuit takes weeks or months, which matters little against a January wall. Little is public about Action Acquisitions, Starman or Midtown, so the proxy will be the first detailed look at the buyer.

The founder is on several sides

Nick Woodman controls the vote through Class B, lent $20 million through affiliated trusts on notes that must be redeemed on a change of control, holds warrants for 25.7 million Class B shares that are cashed out at a Black-Scholes value, and stays on as an officer. An independent committee approved the July financing. The summary does not mention a special committee or a majority-of-the-minority vote for the merger itself. Deals with non-ratable benefits to a controlling holder commonly draw stockholder suits; transaction litigation is excluded from the MAE, so suits would not let the buyer walk, but they could cost time.

The $1.14 is a ceiling

Debt outstanding at signing is excluded from net working capital, so paying it down with cash lowers working capital, and borrowing after signing counts against it. Weekly bank repayments come to about $9 million by year end, on top of operating losses. est. How close that brings GoPro to the adjustment threshold depends on a number that is not public in the materials reviewed.

Employees roll over on better terms than the public

On a literal reading of Section 2.7, each unvested RSU and PSU converts into an award over one surviving-corporation share, while each public share receives 0.1 share plus cash. With about 9.6 million RSUs and up to 4.9 million PSUs outstanding, that could be a large block next to the roughly 20.6 million new shares going to public holders. est. The proxy should explain the intent.

What the stock price implies

Around $1.33 on September 28, the market pays about $0.19 above the cash for 0.1 new share. That implies roughly $1.90 per new share and about $390 million for the combined company, before any public Starman financials. est. Published analyst targets near $0.50 hint at the downside if the deal breaks.
